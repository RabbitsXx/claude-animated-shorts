# Why a Skyscraper Hangs a Giant Golden Ball (working title; final title goes in out/publish/post.txt)

## Facts and sources
- **Fiction:** Pete, Rex, the town, the rival's sabotage, the opening day and the dummy are invented. A tower's own sway is real; the story is a cartoon about it.
- A tuned mass damper is a heavy mass hung inside a tall building that swings against the building's sway and soaks it up. Taipei 101's steel ball weighs about 660 metric tons and hangs between floors 87 and 92 (Guinness World Records "Largest tuned mass damper", https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/774616-largest-tuned-mass-damper ; Mothership, https://mothership.sg/2024/04/taiwan-earthquake-taipei101-damper ; Urban Toronto explainer, https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2016/06/explainer-tuned-mass-damping-counteract-sway). Retrieved 2026-11-09 through a search summary; the pages were not opened directly. Some sources give 728 t for the same ball; the film states no weight.
- The rope length sets how fast a hanging ball swings; the damper works when the ball's swing time matches the building's slow sway (same sources; period of a pendulum 2 pi sqrt(L/g) is standard physics). A taller building sways more slowly, so the ball needs a longer rope.
- Not used: the "up to 40% less sway" figure (the builder's claim, not an independent measurement) and any earthquake claim (the ball's main job is wind sway).
- Everyday picture the film uses: a playground swing. Pushing in time makes it fly; pushing against it stops it.
- Numbers said in the film: none.

## Narration
> Pete just hung a giant golden ball inside his skyscraper.
> You see, the tower is tall and skinny, and it opens tomorrow.
> But the ball ate the penthouse that was going to pay for it.
> So, of course, his rival builds a sleek copy, minus the silly ball.
> But the night before opening, a gale sends his test dummy flying.
> And he can't miss the opening, so he drills through every floor and lowers the ball.
> Does he know what he's doing? Not really.
> His rival laughs, then sneaks in and cranks the winch until the ball hangs deep, so it will smash the floor.
> The next morning, the whole town queues for the rival's rooftop pool.
> Rex rides up first to show off, while Pete steps into his, and the wind arrives.
> As the tower leans, he sees the ball hanging deep. But it's too late.
> You see, a tall tower sways like a slow swing, and pushing back against a swing stops it.
> A ball on a long rope swings slowly, against the tower, which is why the short rope threw the dummy.
> So his coffee never spills, because the ball soaks up every sway.
> And the rival's tower, with no ball, sways until its pool slops over the roof.
> Just as Rex steps out, soaked and seasick.
